Overview
The whole case on one page.
Engineering organisations accumulate hundreds of unowned internal dashboards, ad-hoc scripts, and bespoke admin panels. When database schemas or authentication protocols change, critical operations stall while senior engineers spend hours reverse-engineering orphan tooling.
researcher note · [modeled]
Mid-market software companies (250-2,000 employees) lose over $170,000 annually per 100 engineers in unallocated triage on undocumented internal tools, and will pay $24,000-$48,000/year for automated discovery, schema-drift detection, and lifecycle deprecation; file downgrades if internal developer portals (such as Backstage) successfully automate runtime telemetry without dedicated discovery agents.
The case in 30 seconds
who bleeds Platform engineering leads, Staff DevOps engineers, and VP Engineering at mid-market B2B software companies managing 150+ engineers and multi-year legacy codebases.
the bleed annual engineering payroll lost to fixing and reverse-engineering unmaintained internal tooling per 100 developers
the gap Internal developer portals (Backstage) require manual YAML updates that rot within months; low-code platforms (Retool, Appsmith) isolate apps into proprietary silos; production observability suites (Datadog) are too expensive and complex to instrument onto transient internal scripts.
the wedge Passive GitHub repository and low-code workspace scanner that automatically catalogs internal tools, maps database dependencies, and alerts owners before schema migrations break admin tools.
the endgame The unified governance, runtime observability, and automated deprecation control plane for all enterprise internal software.
the window 18-24 months, before internal developer portal vendors standardise automated schema-drift plugins or low-code suites consolidate enterprise governance.
